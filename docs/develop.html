Developer's guide

A synth for fm1-dock is a module: a small C program built into a .dock file. Players add it to their FM-1 or SMK-37 next to the other synths, without replacing any firmware, and switch to it with HOME. This page is the way from an idea to a synth anyone can add with one click.

  1. 1Write Copy modules/sine/ (150 lines), fill in render() and note(), build it with tools/hbmod.py.
  2. 2Test In the fm1-emu emulator first, then on your own unit: a deploy is a few seconds and changes nothing else.
  3. 3Share Put the .dock in a GitHub release for anyone to deploy, or bring it into the page's catalog for one-click Add.
1

Write a module

What a module is

A table of functions at the start of its image, linked against nothing of the host's. fm1-dock checks its header, ABI and CRC before any of it runs.

  • render() adds 256 stereo frames, Q15, in the audio interrupt (5.8 ms).
  • note(), cc(), ui() and draw() run in the main loop.
  • The host gives the time, the log, the screen, the LEDs, a 240-byte settings record, flash banks for presets, MIDI out and the keys' pressure: every call is in hb_module.h.

Build it

cp -r modules/sine modules/mysynth
# module.cfg:
#   name = MYSYNTH
#   version = 1
python3 tools/hbmod.py modules/mysynth
# -> build/modules/mysynth.dock

A module needs only JieLi's toolchain (tools/get_toolchain.sh), not the SDK; the README's Build it has the setup. The module's directory can live in your own repository (includes = and sources = point outside fm1-dock's tree).

The chip's rules

  • One core at 240 MHz (a second one for voices, optional), integers only, no 64-bit division.
  • 272 KiB of RAM, 16 KiB of code in RAM (HB_FAR).
  • Keep it small: the add-ons share 296 KiB of flash on the FM-1 (from 0.10 more with fewer built-in synths), and most synths take 6 to 60 KiB.
  • The build refuses what would crash the chip: RULES.md.
2

Test it

In the emulator

fm1-emu runs fm1-dock and your module as the chip would, with the screen, the sound and the log; no unit needed, nothing to brick.

tools/dock.py emu \
  --fwsc fm1-dock.fwsc \
  --deploy mysynth.dock \
  --ms 6000

On your FM-1 or SMK-37

With fm1-dock installed: drop the .dock on the console's Deploy a module, or

tools/dock.py deploy \
  mysynth.dock
tools/dock.py logs

A module that crashes twice is skipped at boot until it is picked again, and tools/dock.py logs has the address. Hold OCT+ at power-on for the safe start.

Before you share

  • Full chords at full velocity don't clip: tests/level_test.py plays it in fm1-emu and checks.
  • The CPU on the top line stays under about 80 % with all voices playing.
  • HOME, presets and the settings come back after a power-off.
  • On an SMK-37 too if you can; say so if you couldn't.
3

Share it

Two ways. You can do the first today on your own; the second puts your synth on the page.

A Your own release

  1. Put the source on GitHub with a licence (GPL-3.0, MIT or another open one).
  2. Attach mysynth.dock to a release.
  3. Tell players: install fm1-dock from the page, then drop the .dock on the console's Deploy a module.

No one has to approve it. It doesn't show in the catalog, and players update it by hand.

B In the catalog

Your synth gets a card with Add, Try it in the browser emulator, the room it takes, updates with one click, and a place in the README.

B

Into the catalog

The easy way: an issue

Open an issue titled New module: NAME with a link to your source, its licence, a few lines on what it is, and what you tested it on. We build it, check it in the emulator and on hardware, and add it; you see the pull request.

Or a pull request

  • modules/NAME/: the source and its module.cfg (or a wrapper that builds yours from your repository).
  • docs/modules/NAME.dock: the built module.
  • docs/media/screen_NAME.png: its screen, from the emulator.
  • An entry in docs/modules/modules.json (copy ARP 2600's): title, by, licence, voices, a description, tags, kind.
  • A line in LICENSING.md and the README's table.

What we check

  • An open-source licence, and that you may share everything in it: no manufacturer's ROMs or factory banks.
  • It builds from source, and the .dock matches.
  • It fits next to the others, plays without clipping, and survives HOME and a power-off.
  • Until it has played on a real FM-1, its card says untested.

Port a firmware you already have

Most of the catalog began as its own FM-1 firmware: OberVave, CS-1, AMB-1, FiMba-1, GATOR, PROPHET and others. The synth engine stays as its author wrote it; a small wrapper turns its main loop into render(), note() and ui(), and its screen and buttons into the host's calls.

Look at one

modules/fimba and modules/obervave are small wrappers around their authors' code; modules/cs1 plays half its voices on the second core.

Not your code?

Porting someone else's open-source synth is welcome: keep their copyright and licence, credit them on the card, and tell them. Ask before bringing a firmware whose licence doesn't allow it.

Stuck?

Ask in the issues: a port often needs only a hint about the screen, the banks or the second core.
