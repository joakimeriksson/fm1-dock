ESQ-1 and SQ-80

Ensoniq's ESQ-1 of 1986 and its successor, the SQ-80 of 1987, as one synth in fm1-dock: eight voices of three wavetable oscillators into a four-pole filter, their programs and SysEx, eight tracks over MIDI, and their sequencer playing song files. It is called ESQ-1 on the FM-1 and comes with waves computed to stand in for Ensoniq's, and one song of its own. With the real wave ROMs and the SQ-80's factory disks, which you bring yourself, this page gives it the real waves and plays the factory demos on it.

This browser has no Web MIDI, so it cannot reach the FM-1: open the page in Chrome or Edge on a computer, or Chrome on Android.

On your FM-1

not asked yet

Plug the FM-1 in with a USB data cable, with fm1-dock on it (install it here). Add puts the synth into fm1-dock's flash, or updates it; the other synths and the settings stay.

The Entertainer (Scott Joplin, 1902) is the synth's own song: PLAY on the FM-1 starts it too.

The real waves

stand-in waves

With the ESQ-1's wave ROM among your files (below), this sends it to the synth: the keys and every program play Ensoniq's waves until the FM-1 is switched off. A demo song brings the waves it needs by itself.

Demo songs, step by step

The SQ-80 came with disks of demo songs. This is how to hear them on your FM-1. It takes Chrome or Edge on a computer, a USB data cable, and about five minutes the first time.

  1. Put the synth on the FM-1. With fm1-dock installed and the FM-1 plugged in, press Add ESQ-1 above. The browser asks once to allow MIDI. The Entertainer then plays the synth's own song: if you hear it, the connection works.
  2. Download the four wave ROMs from Rainer Buchty's Ensoniq pages: the four sq80-waverom zips linked below. Do not unzip them.
  3. Download a factory disk, also linked below: vsd-2.gz has eleven songs, vsd-3.gz twenty, vsd-99.gz fourteen. Do not unpack it.
  4. Drop the five files on the box below (or choose them). The page shows the four ROMs as "here" and lists the disk's songs under Songs. They stay in this browser for the next visit.
  5. Press a song. It goes to the FM-1 with its programs and its waves in a few seconds and starts. The FM-1's screen shows the sequencer: the song, the step, the bar, the tempo.
  6. On the FM-1, PLAY stops and starts the song, and the keys play along. Press another song here whenever you like: one song is in the synth at a time.

After the FM-1 has been switched off, the synth is back to its own waves and song: open this page and press a song again. Steps 2 to 4 are done once.

ESQ-1

One wave ROM (number 1) with 32 waves. With that ROM alone, Send the ESQ-1's waves above gives the keys and every program the real ESQ-1 sound. No ESQ-1 demo sequences are known to be on the net as data, so the demo songs here are the SQ-80's.

SQ-80

The same synth with three more ROMs (2, 3 and 4) and 43 more waves: drums, plucked and bowed attacks, voices. Its demo songs use them, so they want all four ROMs; a song brings the tables it plays, and the synth then offers all 75 waves until it is switched off.

Waves and disks

The wave ROMs and the demo songs are Ensoniq's, and are not on this site. Rainer Buchty's Ensoniq pages have them. Download the files and drop them here as they are, zipped: the page unpacks them, keeps them in this browser for the next visit, and sends nothing anywhere but to your FM-1.

  • The four wave ROMs: sq80-waverom-1.zip (the ESQ-1's own), 2, 3, 4 (the SQ-80's three more)
  • The SQ-80's factory disks, with the demo songs and their programs: vsd-2.gz, vsd-3.gz, vsd-99.gz
  • Your own: a ROM dump (2202.bin … 2205.bin, esq1wavlo.bin + esq1wavhi.bin), an SQ80 Toolkit disk dump, a .sqpack

    Help

    After switching off

    The waves and the song a press sent are in the FM-1's memory, not its flash: after a restart the synth has its stand-in waves and The Entertainer again. Open this page and press once more: the files are still in this browser.

    Why one song at a time

    The SQ-80 has 256 KB of waves and a synth in fm1-dock 272 KB of memory in all. So a song comes with the tables its own programs read, and with the ESQ-1's ROM whole when there is room beside them.

    A song sounds wrong

    Without ROMs 2 to 4 the SQ-80's waves (drums, plucked attacks, voices) are silent, and without any ROM everything plays the stand-in waves. The pills above say which ROMs the page has.

    On the FM-1

    SEQ shows the sequencer: the song, its step, the sequence, the bar and the tempo. PLAY starts and stops; SELECT picks another song or sequence while it is stopped.
